Terms of Service

Last updated: August 29, 2026

These terms cover the hosted tools 0200project operates. They are written to be read, not skimmed past. By calling the service, you agree to them.

The service

0200project provides paid, metered API tools intended for programmatic use — by AI agents, scripts, and applications. Today that is base-transaction-decoder, a tool that returns a structured explanation of a Base mainnet transaction. Tools are callable two ways — a plain HTTPS POST to api.0200project.com/explain, or over MCP at /mcp. There are no accounts and no API keys.

Pricing and payment

  • Each IP address gets 50 free calls per rolling 24 hours, shared by everyone behind that address (an office, a VPN exit, a CI runner all draw from the same allowance). No signup is required for them.
  • After that, calls cost $0.02 each, or $9 for a 30-day pass covering 10,000 calls, paid in USDC on Base via the x402 protocol. The payment challenge is embedded in the tool response; a capable client pays and retries on its own.
  • Marketplace-hosted deployments are billed through the marketplace at its rates. The marketplace's billing terms apply there.

Acceptable use

Use the service for what it is: a metered API. Specifically:

  • Do not attempt to circumvent metering, the free tier, or payment.
  • Respect the rate limit of 60 requests per minute per client. Requests beyond it are rejected.
  • Do not attack, probe, or otherwise abuse the service or the infrastructure it runs on.

We may block clients that violate these rules.

No warranty, no SLA

The service is provided as-is, without warranty of any kind. We work to keep it available and correct, but we do not offer a service-level agreement, and the status page is informational, not contractual.

What the output is — and is not

  • The output describes what a transaction did. It is not financial, investment, or legal advice, and it says nothing about whether a transaction was a good idea.
  • Risk flags are evidence-based signals: a flag is only raised when specific evidence was found. They are not guarantees.
  • The absence of a flag is not an endorsement of a transaction or a counterparty, and not a safety guarantee.
